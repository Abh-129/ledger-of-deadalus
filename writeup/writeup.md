*The Labyrinth of Daedalus*

For this project I built *The Labyrinth of Daedalus*, a walkable 3D space on a website in which the Labyrinth itself is made from Daedalus’s own inventions. The visitor follows Ariadne’s thread through six chambers, and each chamber holds one episode of the myth painted as a Minoan fresco [CONFIRM]: the saw invented by Perdix, Daedalus’s twelve-year-old nephew and student; the hollow wooden cow Daedalus builds for Queen Pasiphaë; the Minotaur’s empty chamber; the thread he gives Ariadne; the wings, ending in a short playable flight [CONFIRM]; and the partridge on the beach where Icarus is buried.[^1] As each chamber ends, a line is written into Daedalus’s Ledger, with what the invention solved in ochre and what it cost in red. That ledger carries my interpretation. The usual moral of this myth is aimed at Icarus, who abandons his father’s one instruction before takeoff, “*medio ut limite curras*” (“run the middle path”), and flies too close to the sun.[^2] Read as a ledger, though, the story is about the father. Every invention works exactly as designed, and every one costs someone: the cow produces the Minotaur, the Labyrinth demands fourteen Athenians with every tribute, the thread frees Theseus but gets Daedalus and Icarus locked in his own maze, and the wings carry Daedalus to Sicily but not his son. Two falls frame the whole account, Perdix thrown from the Acropolis at the start and Icarus falling from the sky at the end, and the only inventor left safe is the boy who learned to stay low. By making the visitor walk inside the structure Daedalus built, the project shows him trapped in the consequences of his own skill, the skill Ovid says he cursed at his son’s grave.[^3]

[^1]: Ovid, *Metamorphoses* 8.131-259; Apollodorus, *Library* 3.1.3-4, 3.15.8, and *Epitome* 1.7-15.
[^2]: Ovid, *Metamorphoses* 8.203.
[^3]: Ovid, *Metamorphoses* 8.234.
